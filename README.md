## Colonization Datawork

This data task involves an analysis of GDP per capita trends of multiple colonizer countries vis-à-vis the countries which were colonized.
World share of GDP of these countries is also analyzed. A region wise analysis has also been conducted which uses regions such as MENA, Western Europe,
Southern Europe, European Offshoots, North America, Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America and South Asia to analyse how wealth is distributed across the globe.
Regressions using colonization duration and the colonizer as explanatory variables have also been employed to gauge their associated with the dependent variable
of GDP per capita and GDP gap (difference between the GDP of the colony and the colonizer).

Some results:
- For all colonizers, their colonies have very less GDP compared to colonizers themselves.
- The GDP gap for colonies which were under colonial rule for 0-150 years is very much less than one.
- Similarly, GDP gap for colonies which were under colonial rule for maximum number of centuries (300-400 years) also have a very less (GDPcolony/GDPcolonizer) ratio. 
- Colonization duration overall is negatively associated with GDP gap (favorable for colonies).
- South Asia is also seen to contribute quite a lot to world GDP before 1800s
- European offshoots overtook Western Europe (primarily, the colonizers) after 1800
- At around 1600, India’s and the UK’s GDP per capita was similar. The divergence increased more rapidly post 1800/early 1900s.
